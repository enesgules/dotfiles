Research date: 2026-11-26. Weighting: 2024 to Nov 2026 highest.
Main corpus: his own emails on the kernel lists, 2024-01 to 2026-11. I searched the public-inbox index with `f:torvalds d:20240101..` plus topic words (regression, BUG_ON, WARN_ON, commit message, rebase, too late, AI, special case, churn, garbage, Rust). I fetched 92 of his emails as raw messages and read the parts that he wrote. Other sources: three of his own 2026 commit messages in torvalds/linux, the original parts of `coding-style.rst`, his TED 2016 transcript, and two news reports with direct quotes (LWN on Open Source Summit North America 2026, The Register on Open Source Summit Korea 2025).

## Core values

1. **Do not break what users already use.** "There is *one* major rule in kernel development, and it is "we don't break user space"." ([email, 2026-04-27](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wiLdmz92CCfu2+-9_UrGSn6Pu6sUVvLs5gjyhGaEov-BA@mail.gmail.com/)). "So in the kernel tree, we don't argue about regressions. We fix them." ([email, 2026-01-22](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wheQNiW_WtHGO7bKkT7Uib-p+ai2JP9M+z+FYcZ6CAxYA@mail.gmail.com/)). He says nothing else is as black-and-white: "(ok, the "no regressions" rule comes close, but that's it)." ([email, 2025-09-08](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjutitpc0F8fyCcJhb55UT_sB_5dLNmtxDAVAwxsYSDWw@mail.gmail.com/))
2. **Reality is the contract, not intent, docs, specs, or tests.** "All that matters is reality." ([email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wiLdmz92CCfu2+-9_UrGSn6Pu6sUVvLs5gjyhGaEov-BA@mail.gmail.com/)). "Users are a million times more important than specs or tests." ([email, 2026-03-20](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wh8dnp1qT_CHG-Zz9H2=G1Q=0rF5SYv+YsEaPnVhznxWA@mail.gmail.com/)). "This is why system calls and ABI's need to have hard rules that they actually check, because if they don't, they are stuck with the semantics that people assume." ([email, 2026-04-23](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wh=B3fqU9AaBsyViZR=eVTG5qa2CGyN=qxPROVxu9Gctg@mail.gmail.com/))
3. **A change is allowed. A break that someone notices is not.** "we can try to make any ABI changes at all, and what matters is whether it breaks somebodys setup." and "If people rely on old behavior, it can't change. And if people don't, nobody cares." ([email, 2026-07-29](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjzHTuomhTegkD69veDVRxprX8NB=i7A+BEB2-v6v5n+g@mail.gmail.com/)). A deliberate change must be visible: "And please make sure that any changed behaviour is really obvious, so that people don't spend lots of time chasing down *why* something broke." (same [email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjzHTuomhTegkD69veDVRxprX8NB=i7A+BEB2-v6v5n+g@mail.gmail.com/))
4. **A message must explain why, and must be true.** "Again - you're explaining what it *does*.  I see what it does." and "What I'm asking for is *WHY*." ([email, 2025-03-26](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wiH3hoPTxX3=xTRzRuCwktf3pNzFWP45-x6AwoVAjUsUQ@mail.gmail.com/)). A message that does not match the diff is worse than none: "My complaint isn't about the level of detail." and it "does not match the actual patch in any way, shape, or form." ([email, 2024-01-23](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wgMPVv-mDxA2qcywpLCRLojtaKmP13h7bVo4m=XN202xA@mail.gmail.com/))
5. **History is a record. Do not rewrite it to look clean.** "No. The fix is to NOT REBASE POINTLESSLY." ([email, 2026-10-29](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wiXTPC_jJ9o7k2JGVwA=F5_JFt4ikY2J4E69xJqYtAFGg@mail.gmail.com/)). "So "it has a conflict" is real information about the development process, and shouldn't be hidden." ([email, 2025-08-03](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=whbY2xZpj6c-vWG0qeiDVpGa6SLA8DqqAHP2S0mu3b_pA@mail.gmail.com/))
6. **Fixes add bugs too. Late changes must be small.** "This whole notion of "anything that is a fix goes in -rc" is bogus." and "And that makes the *fixes* possibly buggy." ([email, 2026-11-24](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=whyUfwzMDMTpih8Xs1MxmhwTCDAc_YwUSZG79vXFMLU=Q@mail.gmail.com/)). "We're not in that kind of a hurry, and hurried cleanups aren't great." ([email, 2025-12-01](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wgir86nTfX8SG05QhTtV-Vghkk-q6RMeiBUb80hSF2+Lg@mail.gmail.com/))
7. **Good taste: make the special case go away.** "sometimes you can see a problem in a different way and rewrite it so that a special case goes away and becomes the normal case. And that's good code." ([TED 2016](https://www.ted.com/talks/linus_torvalds_the_mind_behind_linux/transcript)). In 2025: "And honestly, if you worry so much about it, just allow the zero-sized case. I don't see why you want to special-case it in the first place." ([email, 2025-02-09](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=whirZek1fZQ_gYGHZU71+UKDMa_MYWB5RzhP_owcjAopw@mail.gmail.com/)). In 2026: "This still seems very hacky and a complete special case for one odd situation." ([email, 2026-05-15](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wiHfzbufXD2nuc9BhOVp-pYEmnVUYeThKmfZRXLU0kJ7w@mail.gmail.com/))
8. **Crashing is not safety. Errors are handled.** "Error handling is a thing - BUG_ON() is *not* error handling." ([email, 2026-04-13](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wgerpUKCDhdzKH0FEdLyfhj3doc9t+kO9Yb6rSsTp7hdQ@mail.gmail.com/)). "A dead machine is not a safe machine." ([email, 2026-03-31](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wg=q7ptcMyUAqPGDbX_DKHivJVppc8bP0zpQqzOky_avA@mail.gmail.com/)). "I really think that the *ONLY* situation where BUG() is valid is when you absolutely *know* that corruption will happen, and you cannot continue." ([email, 2025-06-04](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjO1xL_ZRKUG_SJuh6sPTQ-6Lem3a3pGoo26CXEsx_w0g@mail.gmail.com/))
9. **Understand the result, whatever tool wrote it.** "The kind of expectations *I* have is that people do their best and I expect people to understand what they are doing." and "But that understanding has very little to do with what tools they use." ([email, 2026-10-13](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=whTs09_ozN+e09GU68N1wPP56MUzDjrg9rnKxQUa0A_4Q@mail.gmail.com/)). From a news report (LWN): "you need to understand not just your prompts, but you need to understand your end result, because that's the only way you can maintain it long term" ([LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/1073761/))

## Review checklist (what he checks or pushes back on)

1. **Does it break something that worked?**
   - A fix that breaks other users is invalid: "a fix that causes regressions is BY DEFINITION not a fix at all." ([email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wh8dnp1qT_CHG-Zz9H2=G1Q=0rF5SYv+YsEaPnVhznxWA@mail.gmail.com/))
   - "It may be true, but it's irrelevant for the people whose binaries no longer work." ([email, 2026-04-23](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjsEqWJxXArS-P5+ksSY2Apaox8u6FtUrtyWSBEOL4Q2Q@mail.gmail.com/))
   - Why the rule exists: "it's better to have a stable base with known problems - that you can perhaps document - than have a base where the problems just shift around like quicksand" ([email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wh8dnp1qT_CHG-Zz9H2=G1Q=0rF5SYv+YsEaPnVhznxWA@mail.gmail.com/))
2. **Is the contract real use, or only intent?** "Any "intent" that isn't actually enforced by _checking_ for it is only wishful thinking, and worth nothing at all." ([email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wiLdmz92CCfu2+-9_UrGSn6Pu6sUVvLs5gjyhGaEov-BA@mail.gmail.com/)). Inference: if a rule matters, the code must reject bad input. If it does not reject it, callers will depend on the loose behavior.
3. **Is a test failure the same as a user regression?** No. "a test-suite error isn't necessarily where you have to draw the line - it's a big red flag" ([email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wheQNiW_WtHGO7bKkT7Uib-p+ai2JP9M+z+FYcZ6CAxYA@mail.gmail.com/)). But "Users complaining is the only real line in the end." (same [email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wheQNiW_WtHGO7bKkT7Uib-p+ai2JP9M+z+FYcZ6CAxYA@mail.gmail.com/))
4. **If a change is user-visible, is the failure loud for the user?** "kernel developers may think that kernel logs are important, but users seldom even think about them." He suggests a returned error instead of a log line ([email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjzHTuomhTegkD69veDVRxprX8NB=i7A+BEB2-v6v5n+g@mail.gmail.com/)).
5. **Fix or revert, but do not ship a known regression.** "And if fixes look too scary or uncertain, we *revert* the change that caused a regression." and "Because no, "let's just have a known regression" is not how we roll." ([email, 2026-04-02](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wiNdTNNxzRuxXYq1Ebpiv6YF4UMZApoQGnktbMjKFYP+w@mail.gmail.com/)). When the cause is unclear: "it's probably worth reverting simply to get time to think about the issue more." ([email, 2026-03-21](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=whFuDumk75VTTWeXzGfCQae4=XT-k8MdYWrca5JtcMrBw@mail.gmail.com/))
6. **Does the message say why?** "The commit message doesn't actually make any mention of *why* any of this would be a good idea." ([email, 2025-03-25](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjbahY4JjLCXNT4o3xVq2cejqBG69z+MNfHsN9FQBnbOw@mail.gmail.com/)). For a bug fix: "when fixing outright bugs, at least document what went wrong and why." ([email, 2025-08-10](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjn5AtuNixX36qDGWumG4LiSDuuqfbaGH2RZu2ThXzV-A@mail.gmail.com/))
7. **Does the message prove its claim, or only guess?** "This explanation makes me nervous." and "No "should be NULL", in other words." ([email, 2026-03-06](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wiFtUn0yxQ6SicnHrkuXfVB4bh1akVd+ryhiqBYodPYSw@mail.gmail.com/))
8. **Is the message honest about whose bug it is?** "There's no point in claiming this fixes a kernel bug when it's a pure workaround for a bug elsewhere." ([email, 2026-02-06](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjzFZMAoJkrOg7LWsQcVOuZhOgMFWGC-sBHOACE8B4+Mw@mail.gmail.com/))
9. **Can the message stand alone?** "The commit log is supposed to be legible without a network connection." ([email, 2026-07-27](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wj9JjzWfnoscfj7xg5NrHswXtU+sjZjrO4kcnwE_ET3Wg@mail.gmail.com/)). If there is a link, "Make the *first* link be something useful, like the *reason* for the patch in the first place." ([email, 2025-04-02](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjcYW9Kf6Yrua_+RqB32RB9aVnvwzzXEC_6sdWinA1AoQ@mail.gmail.com/))
10. **Will it help the person who bisects into it?** "explaining to somebody who is in the middle of a bisect" and "The difference between a shortlog and a commit message is that the commit message should *explain* what is going on." ([email, 2025-08-04](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjxPV1NGZokCyg2su=y1nmJFXTb4asrosw_W+yyqNaq_w@mail.gmail.com/))
11. **Does each commit build on its own?** "Just *remove* the patches that broke the build and tried to deal with the merge ahead of time. It needs to build for *you*, not for me." ([email, 2026-04-16](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wink=Byr72DSJq3DMR9pceuS9s=0ePAxfob-D4oa64mAw@mail.gmail.com/))
12. **Revert-then-reapply, or broken-then-reverted, in one branch.** "The complete mindlessness of that "revert only to reapply" really almost made me throw this out." ([email, 2025-06-06](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wh3sbe9ZU_-q4Et=OWtedfFoB2tsZy_+ssMRZnsjApYhw@mail.gmail.com/)). For a failed series: "So I really feel like at that point you go "this branch was a failure" and start anew - aka rebase." ([email, 2024-01-17](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wgSF9zaUCMWe8FuLrfwS0PxJXyxQxFGn7_W22C0rTaQXQ@mail.gmail.com/))
13. **Was tested history thrown away?** "You threw away any and all testing that had been done by anybody else in linux-next." ([email, 2026-04-19](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjaGOBCsRpEhmPLcsuK6ggiaaeyz0rT6x3Zv7DMK7JNHA@mail.gmail.com/)). "please don't rebase over unimportant stylistic issues." ([email, 2024-10-10](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wgBmW4hs+6PwKNDx8uwhOE2arV9FhuwSOp3_mBuL6wnSw@mail.gmail.com/))
14. **Is a late change only the fix?** "So they clearly aren't regression fixes. Why are they in an -rc at all then?" ([email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wiSnTE9vBZ=5_v+3EEkdazCCbBM5YABRzRHUAeRdyd4Xw@mail.gmail.com/)). About new tests in a fix pull: "You should have kept your tests in a separate branch for your testing, and done this *huge* update for the next merge window." ([email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=whyUfwzMDMTpih8Xs1MxmhwTCDAc_YwUSZG79vXFMLU=Q@mail.gmail.com/))
15. **Too much churn at once.** "This is all old code that didn't need to be changed all at once." ([email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wgir86nTfX8SG05QhTtV-Vghkk-q6RMeiBUb80hSF2+Lg@mail.gmail.com/)). One branch, one thing: "when the individual pulls are smaller and have clear "this branch does XYZ and nothing more", it turns out that the actual effort per pull ends up being less" ([email, 2024-03-16](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wgKJti5WBi7VmA_ETDiXjmkEqvVW7De5ajwtkyJ=c==kA@mail.gmail.com/))
16. **A special case added for one caller.** "Trying to make it specific to one special freeing path only makes it fragile and less obvious" ([email, 2026-11-09](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=whnH5qLJH3Wd3bOxv--Ei6674Jo4iifGD_MqzyJg2Jm4g@mail.gmail.com/)). "So this basically takes a audit mistake, and makes that mistake visible to non-audit users." ([email, 2026-04-13](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wiW53j3vmc1Y58-E_8jUBJtjgAVxDRt+r-w3WPQN+Zm5w@mail.gmail.com/))
17. **A bigger bandaid instead of the fix.** "And the fix is *NOT* to make the bandaid bigger, but to just fix the things that are broken." ([email, 2026-05-16](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wgvUW=1qtJxYcvbA_WaTom6n73nT7S_=7tZd0bo49BNOA@mail.gmail.com/)). "fix the problem spot, don't add new horrid logic elsewhere." ([email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wiHfzbufXD2nuc9BhOVp-pYEmnVUYeThKmfZRXLU0kJ7w@mail.gmail.com/)). Changed in the refresh: the first pass gave the question of where the fix belongs to the Mitchell seat. The replay showed that the question *is the fix where the bug lives?* is his first point in most patch reviews, so the seat now asks it as a change-level question. Module design stays with Mitchell. See the section Patch reviews (last year).
18. **A helper that hides the meaning.** "If you write the code out as "(a << 16) + b", you know what it does and which is the high word." ([email, 2025-08-09](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjLCqUUWd8DzG+xsOn-yVL0Q=O35U9D6j6=2DUWX52ghQ@mail.gmail.com/)). But a helper that names a repeated pattern is fine: "needs an explanation, and probably a helper." ([email, 2024-11-18](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wgqUNhk8awrnf+WaJQc9henwvXsYTyLbF2UFSL7vCuVyg@mail.gmail.com/))
19. **Magic numbers that the code does not derive.** "and then made the code match the result they wanted, but without making the code show how they got there." ([email, 2026-04-13](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjTfaTPf+Vp=XGmW1z9YPZcx8dhPjaGfhfYett9=-jHMA@mail.gmail.com/)). "Having very random constants in header files with no explanation for them is not great." ([email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjn5AtuNixX36qDGWumG4LiSDuuqfbaGH2RZu2ThXzV-A@mail.gmail.com/))
20. **Crash or error?**
   - "Let's just return errors from this, and put the WARN_ON() in the caller." and "You want the warnings to be where the *problem* is. Not in the helper that did everything right, but was passed the wrong values." ([email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjTfaTPf+Vp=XGmW1z9YPZcx8dhPjaGfhfYett9=-jHMA@mail.gmail.com/))
   - "No BUG_ON(), but also no endless loop." and "Just return NULL for bogus users. Really. Give a WARN_ON_ONCE() to make it easy to find offenders, and then let them deal with it." ([email, 2024-08-22](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wiyq9vF-L7NXakadGdQ4TvFLrEwds-NzkByDSb2F2YUkQ@mail.gmail.com/))
   - "it really is absolutely *horrendously* bad for the case where the code hits actual users." ([email, 2025-09-20](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wiXPnY9vWFC87sHudSDYY+wpfTrs-uxd7DBypeE+15Y0g@mail.gmail.com/))
21. **Whose bug is it?** "The bug isn't in the VM. It's in the caller. Don't take on other peoples problems." ([email, 2024-08-19](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjaV+jNdKh26ZEkzRcpbxQCzZbPMsWn1+AM-tCxc6s44w@mail.gmail.com/))
22. **Code that silently corrects bad input.** "I really think that ALIGN() is absolutely the opposite of robust. It silently just makes incorrect values generate incorrect code." and "Robust is dealing correctly with incorrect values, not making it incorrect data just create even more incorrect data." ([email, 2025-03-31](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wi5pLoe3szxLREQGGJuWU0w_POK9Sv6717UH3b7OvvfjQ@mail.gmail.com/))
23. **Guess-work checks and warnings.** "It's going to cause more bugs, it's going to only work for the validation scenarios you thought about, and it's going to make it harder to debug the cases it actually catches." ([email, 2025-04-02](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjAW=WHnmP3+B8dF_U91FEzkesUW0JPGMsip2CJ9e0hgw@mail.gmail.com/)). "Adding a WARN_ON is just making the code worse." His two good options: "(a) convince yourself that the length really can never be true, and you write a comment about why that is the case." or keep the plain handling ([email, 2025-02-09](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wihBAcJLiC9dxj1M8AKHpdvrRneNk3=s-Rt-Hv5ikqo4g@mail.gmail.com/)).
24. **Removing "obsolete" code: is the reason really gone?** "It's not entirely clear to me that the original reason for this macro is actually gone." ([email, 2026-09-04](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wiKvgXTJAXveUoWq0+Wn=E5255-Uc5ZzhXDgk31iXEZgw@mail.gmail.com/)). "But we used your trick for a reason: it fixed a real and present issue." ## Tension: "we don't break user space" against the user's no-compat rule

The user's rule:

> Do not preserve backward compatibility. Remove obsolete paths instead of adding compatibility layers, fallbacks, or migrations.

**Where he agrees with the user.**
- Change is allowed. Only a break that someone notices counts: "If people rely on old behavior, it can't change. And if people don't, nobody cares." ([email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjzHTuomhTegkD69veDVRxprX8NB=i7A+BEB2-v6v5n+g@mail.gmail.com/))
- He tells people not to avoid change out of fear: "So everybody should typically avoid changing user-visible things because it's potentially painful and developer-intensive, but not because it can't be done." (same [email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjzHTuomhTegkD69veDVRxprX8NB=i7A+BEB2-v6v5n+g@mail.gmail.com/))
- He removes code that defends a false rule: "the debug code in question needs to just be removed, since it's now actively detrimental" ([email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wjsEqWJxXArS-P5+ksSY2Apaox8u6FtUrtyWSBEOL4Q2Q@mail.gmail.com/)). And: "I think the goal here should be "minimal fix" followed by "remove that horrendous thing"." ([email, 2024-01-24](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=whq+Kn-_LTvu8naGqtN5iK0c48L1mroyoGYuq_DgFEC7g@mail.gmail.com/))
- He makes deliberate user-visible restrictions when he thinks nobody relies on the old behavior, and he says so in the commit: "If anybody notices, Lorenzo will be boarding Flying Pig Airlines." ([commit 0b2758f48f22](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/commit/0b2758f48f22b173963f39e553d0ecd05f3b4433))
- A late regression report can flip the answer: "if the regression reports then come in late, people may have started relying on the new behavior, and reverting things then causes its own set of regressions." ([email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wgryF_COh9xzhGaJ-FAm471pn5uvqt-Z94+3ENPVu4w-Q@mail.gmail.com/))

**Where he conflicts with the user.**
- "If it used to work, and people relied on it, you add a new "right" format, and keep the old one around." ([email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wheQNiW_WtHGO7bKkT7Uib-p+ai2JP9M+z+FYcZ6CAxYA@mail.gmail.com/)). This is a compatibility layer. The user's rule says no.
- Inference: the kernel has millions of users it cannot contact and binaries it cannot rebuild. The user's apps have one owner, few or known clients, and deploys that can roll back in minutes. The cost of keeping old paths is higher for the user, and the cost of a break is lower and easier to see.

**How the seat adapts it (proposal).**
1. **Internal code: the user's rule wins.** Internal functions, modules, types, database columns that only the app reads, and private endpoints have no compatibility promise. Delete old paths. Do not ask for a shim, an alias, a fallback, or a deprecation period.
2. **Behavior a real user or client depends on: a break must be deliberate and stated.** Examples: public URLs and redirects, API response shapes and status codes used by other clients, webhook payloads, stored user data, sessions, and the API that shipped mobile app versions call. The seat does not ask for a compat layer. It asks the PR to say: what breaks, for whom, why that is acceptable, and how to revert.
3. **The failure must be loud.** A removed field or route gives a clear error, not a silent default or a log line that nobody reads (from his point about kernel logs above).
4. **Evidence over belief.** A claim that nobody uses it needs a check: a code search, logs, analytics, or the list of clients. Docs and intent do not count (core value 2).
5. **If a real user reports the break, fix or revert first.** Do not argue. A revert of the bad deploy is not a compat layer. It is the fix preference below.
6. **A silent break is a `block`.** A stated, checked break is fine and goes in `Checked clean`.

## Heuristics that generalize (incl. TypeScript web apps)

Each item names its source. The translation to TypeScript web apps is my inference.

1. **List what users can observe.** For each change, list the observable surfaces it touches: route paths, redirects, query params, response JSON shape, status codes, error codes, cookie names, stored data format, webhook payloads, emails sent, and the API calls of shipped Expo app versions. Each change there needs the statement from the tension section. (regression emails, 2026)
2. **A schema, lint, or test fix can be a regression.** Making a handler match a Zod schema or a spec, or making a test pass, can break a client that relied on the old shape. Ask who calls it. (bluetooth email, 2026-03-20)
3. **Validate what you promise.** If a field or flag must have certain values, reject other values now (Zod `strict()`, `z.enum`). Loose input that is accepted today becomes a contract tomorrow. (rseq emails, 2026-04)
4. **Revert first when the cause is unclear.** For a production regression, revert the deploy or the commit, then fix with time to think. Apply a direct fix only when it is obvious. (bluetooth email, 2026-03-21; amdgpu email, 2026-04-02)
5. **A hotfix PR holds only the fix.** No refactor, rename, formatting, new test suite, or codemod in the same PR. Those go in the next PR. An AI-found old bug that no user hit is not urgent: do not put it in a release-day change. (emails 2026-09-24 and 2026-11-24)
6. **Commit messages: why, proof, and honesty.** Say why the change exists. Replace `should` with `is always X because Y`. Name a workaround for a library or platform bug as a workaround, with the library version. Do not explain only with a link. For agent-made or codemod changes, name the tool and give the prompt or script. (selinux, osq, vmware, KEYS, tooling emails)
7. **History: do not rewrite what others have.** Do not force-push a shared branch. Do not rebase only to resolve a conflict or to look clean after testing. Squash a revert-then-reapply. Every commit builds and passes, so `git bisect` works. Cleaning up a private branch before review is fine: "Rebasing is for *important* things, or for early development." ([email, 2025-12-12](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wh-LQTLhD-vOqHfhb3cyust1aeU4UPmTyGkaD5kgDFLzg@mail.gmail.com/)) (rebase emails)
8. **Make the special case the normal case.** Examples: an `if (items.length === 0) return []` in front of code that already handles an empty list; first-item vs other-item branches; an `isFirstPage` or `isAdmin` branch that one data shape could remove. The best diff deletes the branch. (TED; pipe email; sched/wait patches)
9. **Do not spread one caller's problem.** When one caller needs odd behavior, do not add a flag or branch to the shared helper. Fix that caller. (RCU email; fs_struct email; ptrace email)
10. **Failure scope.** One bad request, row, or message fails alone. It does not crash the Worker isolate, the server process, or the whole batch. In Effect, use typed errors (`Effect.fail`) for states that can happen, and defects (`Effect.die`, `orDie`) only for true corruption. In React, an error boundary keeps one widget from blanking the page. Watch for `!`, `as`, `throw` on an unknown enum value from the database, and unhandled promise rejections. (BUG_ON emails)
11. **Error at the boundary, warning at the caller.** A shared helper returns an error for bad input. It does not log for every caller, crash, or loop forever. The caller that passed bad data owns the fix. (smb3 and nofail emails)
12. **Do not silently correct bad input.** A clamp, a round, a `?? default`, or a `Math.max(0, x)` in a shared helper turns a caller bug into wrong data. Reject the bad value instead. (ALIGN email)
13. **No guess-work guards.** Either prove that a case cannot happen and write the reason in a comment, or handle it as a normal case. Do not add `console.warn('should never happen')`. (pipe and tracing emails)
14. **Removing obsolete code: check the original reason.** Before you delete a workaround, find why it was added (git blame, the old commit message). Delete it when the reason is gone, and say so in the message. (RELOC_HIDE and gcc emails)
15. **Use the compiler to find every use.** Rename a field or function locally, let `tsc` list every use, fix each one, then rename back if the name should stay. Write `all N uses checked` in the message. This is a good check for agent changes. (comm_str email)
16. **Review conduct.** Do not block a merge on nits. Do not ask the author to fix old problems found nearby: file them separately. Give the alternative as a code sketch. (nit email; AI review email)
17. **AI-made changes meet the same bar.** The author must understand the result. A large change that is hard to verify needs a way to verify it (types, a script, a small diff per commit), or it does not merge. (trust and tooling emails; LWN report)
